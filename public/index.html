3 values disagree with the buyer

One order, three answers.

The tech pack says how to make the garment, the purchase order says what the buyer bought, and the factory sheet says what the line will actually cut. Nobody owns the space between them.

So someone opens all three, scrolls, and compares by eye — at the end of a long day, on the fourth order of the morning. The numbers that disagree are the ones nobody is looking for.

How SpecCheck works.

01

Upload

Drop the tech pack, the purchase order and the factory sheet — PDF, spreadsheet, or pasted text.

02

Extract

Each document is read into the same fields: colour, composition, quantity, ship date, measurements, care label, trims. Blank stays blank.

03

Compare

Values are normalised and compared field by field in code — units, dates, formatting — then the model judges what is a real difference.

04

Flag

Conflicts and gaps come back ranked High, Medium, Low, with the value from each document and the line it came from.

05

Decide

Fix needed, ignore, or ask the supplier. You record the call and export the report. Nothing is approved for you.

The words on the documents.

Tech pack
The construction file: measurements, fabric, trims, labelling, how the garment is built.
Purchase order
The commercial instruction from the buyer: quantity, price, colour, ship date, destination.
Factory sheet
What production plans to cut and sew, written by the factory from the two documents above.
Merchandiser
The person who holds the order together between buyer and factory, and owns the fix.
Spec
Any single instruction that can be checked: a measurement, a colour, a fibre content, a date.
Flag
A place where two documents cannot both be right — or where one is silent and should not be.

AI reads and compares. A human always makes the final call.

SpecCheck never approves an order, never edits a document and never contacts a supplier. It shows you where the three disagree and keeps a record of the decision you made.
